# Estar Prompt 4 Fresh Trial Comparison

The fresh trial reached an independent Send as Needs Information verdict after one package correction, without a substantive prose revision from the independent reviewer. The earlier final draft also reached Send as Needs Information. This is one observed production run, not proof that the new standards yield better prose or a reliable unattended process.

| Aspect | Earlier final draft | Fresh trial |
|---|---|---|
| Production inputs | Accumulated research and operator editorial corrections | Original prompt, website, supplied reference, current Writing Standards and fresh research |
| Isolation | Earlier drafting history present | Producer and reviewer reported no access to previous article, research or critiques |
| Provider uncertainty | Visible required-fact marker asking who performs each procedure | Supported provider roles and team invitation; no procedure mapping claimed or needed |
| Unresolved required input | Keywords unknown in comments; provider marker in body | Keywords and metadata scope visibly marked beside title and anchored customer question |
| Optional material | Product confirmation, quote, prices, review/social comments | Quote and price comments; other features assessed for applicability rather than automatically requested |
| Article shape | Comparison table and ten H2 sections | Nine H2 sections and short goal/cost question lists |
| Independent assessment | Focused prose revision required for second draft; final third draft sendable | Initial prose sendable; visible-input marker required for review package |
| Local deliverable | Four-page Word file with six comments | Four-page Word file with four comments |

The clearest difference is the required-versus-optional dependency judgment. Missing clinician-to-procedure associations no longer force a body placeholder when the supported article makes no such association. Unknown keywords still prevent a full compliance claim. Both articles remain roughly 1,400 words and depend on Alex's review for customer acceptance.

A remaining editorial concern for comparison is consultation-heavy wording. The fresh article often tells the reader to ask the injector, and its swelling timing is less concrete than the previous article. The fresh reviewer treated a supported recovery window as an optional improvement. Neither review establishes that the fresh version is better for readers overall.

The author performed readability revisions and proofreading before independent review. The original candidate is preserved as article.md; article-reviewed.md differs only by the missing-input note. Word text, hyperlinks and four comment anchors match the reviewed package. No Google Doc, Sheet entry, customer delivery, clinical expert validation or acceptance is claimed.

This is a whole-method trial with fresh research and model judgment. It does not isolate the causal effect of rewriting Writing Standards. The original trial artifacts remain unchanged. The source-archive authority proposal remains pending approval and was not applied; the operator's direct instruction to use Writing Standards without reconstructing archived rules was supplied to both fresh contexts.
