Initial draft: 1,431 body words; estimated Flesch-Kincaid grade 9.53; 44 of 45 prose paragraphs one or two sentences. Evidence and structure checks found no unsupported central premise. The first readability estimate was above the aim of grades six through nine.

Revisions: simplified 23 dense passages without changing the central answer; split long sentences about off-label status, HA, product choice, reversal, aftercare, consultation, and timing. Replaced abstract terms such as “reproduced,” “interchangeable,” and “contribute” with direct explanations. Kept technical names where needed, explained HA and off-label use, and separated 10 paragraph boundaries for scanning. The final proofread removed four redundant passages about exact product choice, photos, settled swelling, and removal risks. No extra medical claim, link, numerical claim, provider association, price, or testimonial was introduced. Final body count 1,403; grade estimate 8.02; average sentence length 13.36 words; 48 of 53 prose paragraphs one or two sentences.

The numeric scan found only 12 in the qualified duration and the verified phone digits. One through nine are spelled out. No date, percent, or price assertion needs a formatting check. Business names and professional-title capitalization were proofread; MD is the practice's actual state, not an inferred extra location. Oxford commas appear in the parallel lists. AP compound modifiers include fuller-looking, natural-looking, FDA-approved, board-certified, before-and-after, and muscle-relaxing.

## Claim-to-Evidence Reconciliation

Evidence IDs refer to research.md. All factual claim groups in the final article were assessed, not only linked passages.

- Introduction and lip flip section: M1 establishes muscle relaxation/exposure without added volume; practice service page establishes availability; M4 indication list supports off-label characterization. No dosage or FDA lip-flip efficacy claim.
- Filler section: M2/M5 establish HA gel/volume and shape; Estar lip filler page establishes local HA emphasis. M3/M6/M7 support site/product specificity; no specific stock promise.
- Goal section: choice criteria derive from mechanism and the patient's stated goals; photo and question-list advice is editorial planning, not a clinical outcome claim. Combination is a discussion prompt supported by first-party clinical educational coverage in AAFE, not a recommendation that both treatments are indicated.
- Timing: M1 supports about-week onset and temporary effect. M2 supports swelling and later assessment. M5 supports approximate six-to-12-month HA range; M3 qualifies material/site differences. M2's 12-to-18-month generic range was not silently combined into a false precise estimate. Article explicitly states range varies; no treatment deadline promised.
- Risks: M1 supports temporary function issues; M4 supports urgent toxin warning signs; M3 supports ordinary filler effects, delayed reactions, rare vascular harms, and urgent symptom wording. Article distinguishes lip-control effects from emergency symptoms rather than minimizing both as routine.
- Reversal: M1 supports waiting for toxin effect; M2 supports HA enzyme dissolution; M3 supports removal risks/limitations. No guarantee of return to baseline or complete rescue from vascular harm.
- Costs: no actual prices or universal price comparison. Questions and a conditional lower-initial-fee example provide planning, not a local fee claim. No claim that combining treatments saves money.
- Consultation/providers: Estar home/about support care approach, roles and credentials; M4/M6/M7 support health-history review. No certification independently verified beyond attributed practice evidence, no claimed specialist board credential, no procedure mapping. CTA phone matches practice home and service pages.
