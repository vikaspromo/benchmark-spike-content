# Writing Standards

Writing Standards owns the research, content, evidence, structure, language, and formatting standards that assigned writing must satisfy. It defines required and conditional standards, applicability, and approved interpretations and exceptions.

Read the sections governing the article and its missing inputs. Product owns system purpose and assignment scope; User Design owns product-user behavior, delivery, missing-information presentation, and customer review; Data Model owns record meanings and valid associations; Architecture owns the production flow; Engineering owns verification methods and evidence; Release owns deployment and recovery; AGENTS owns agent responsibilities, authorization, and governing-document changes.

Writing Standards is the authoritative writing specification. Architecture applies these standards during production, Engineering verifies compliance, and User Design defines how articles and unresolved information reach Alex. Agents use this document to determine writing requirements.

## Authority and Requirement Tiers

Follow the requirements stated in this document for article production and evaluation. Historical source material is retained in `sources/writing-standards/` for provenance and requested document revisions. Do not consult that archive to derive additional writing rules, reinterpret these standards, or resolve gaps during article production. Supplied article briefs, client websites, and topic research remain production inputs under Architecture; their factual use does not give historical writing instructions governing authority.

This document can change through the governing-document approval process independently of the archive. Changes do not require matching edits to historical sources. A source disagreement does not override an approved requirement here. Bring an unresolved requirement or proposed change to the operator under AGENTS rather than independently applying a different source interpretation.

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Required standard | Follow the stated rule with its conditions and exceptions. |
| Explicit override | Apply the stated replacement of another rule within its defined scope. |
| Conditional requirement | Apply when relevant to the article and supported. Record applicability and material availability rather than treating the feature as universally required. |
| Optional enhancement | Useful material that is unavailable or not needed for the article's answer. Its absence alone does not prevent Ready for Review. |

Use the article's reader question to determine relevance. Preserve explicit requirements and exceptions. Do not turn examples or useful details into universal requirements. For conditional requirements, record whether they apply, whether supporting material is available, and whether the article includes or omits the material.

An article-specific client instruction overrides the affected standard when the instruction explicitly requests a different treatment. Keep that override distinct from the general rule. Do not silently replace the assigned topic, invent requirements, or report an unavailable input as a passed check. Resolve ambiguous instructions with the operator or Alex as appropriate under AGENTS and User Design.

## Reader Answer and Substantive Content

Answer the assigned question clearly, accurately, and thoroughly for the intended reader. Give supported explanations, practical expectations, and distinctions that help the reader understand the topic or make the decision the article addresses. Demonstrate familiarity with reader concerns, use trusted evidence, and avoid exaggerated claims or unsupported promises. Relevance, accuracy, useful explanations, and meaningful practice customization guide the selection and writing of content.

Explain what research can establish before referring the reader to a provider. Reserve consultation advice for individual suitability, a personal treatment plan, or another decision that needs individual assessment. Do not use repeated instructions to ask a provider in place of supported answers the article can give. Keep appropriate safety advice and individualized medical limits explicit.

Each section must answer the question or address the subject stated in its heading and add distinct reader value. Every paragraph, bullet, and example must contribute directly to that answer through an explanation, evidence, a relevant distinction, or a necessary qualification. Move useful material to its proper section; remove tangents, repetition, and material that adds no information needed for the reader's understanding. Preserve necessary medical qualifications and safety advice under appropriate headings. Short paragraphs and a readable formula score do not establish useful content.

The final article in the initial workload must contain 1,150–1,250 words of substantive body content, including the introduction, bullets, and closing call to action. Exclude the title, headings, and document comments from the count. This operator-approved range replaces the historical 1,200-word minimum and shorter lengths printed beside assignment prompts. Quality guides coverage and writing; word count confirms the resulting article's length rather than determining section content or word allocations. Never add filler or remove necessary information to meet the range. [Architecture](architecture.md#evaluate-and-revise) defines revision when the resulting draft falls outside the range; [Engineering](engineering.md#article-verification) defines final measurement and verification.

Produce localized, treatment-specific education with useful subtopics, natural practice integration, authoritative support, and a localized call to action. Choose the structure for the article's reader question. Product owns the Benchmark writer-quality goal; User Design owns Alex's review and acceptance.

## Practice Research and Provider Coverage

Customize each article for the actual client using supported practice details that explain the topic, available treatment, patient experience, or consultation choice. General educational explanations may apply across practices. The article must also contain substantive passages that would need factual rewriting if another practice's names, locations, and links were substituted. Those passages must reflect supported facts about this practice and help answer the assigned question. Provider biographies and service links must contribute relevant context; interchangeable names, credentials, links, and cities alone do not establish customization. No fixed proportion of practice-specific content is required. Do not force practice details into sections where they add no reader value or invent differences between practices.

Research the client's own website before using third-party sources. Establish the practice context relevant to this article, including providers, credentials, treatments, technology, philosophy, locations, differentiators, terminology, and client writing preferences. The article need not contain every research subject. Treat supplied reference URLs as research starting points rather than material to rewrite or the only sources to consult. [Architecture](architecture.md#understand-the-practice) defines the practice research stage. Preserve the correct person, credential, service, treatment association, physical location, and target geography under Data Model.

Include providers whose supported roles or expertise help explain the topic or consultation choice. Avoid focusing arbitrarily on one person when several are relevant, unless the prompt requires that individual. Relevant provider coverage does not require a complete practice roster or procedure-by-provider mapping. Confirm a procedure-provider association before making that claim; do not require the association when supported wording satisfies the article's purpose without it.

## Voice, Introduction, and Structure

Use conversational language, active voice, clear explanations, natural transitions, and real-world examples when appropriate. Avoid repetitive wording, keyword stuffing, generic AI phrases, unnecessary technical language, long walls of text, excessive keyword bolding, and introductions that immediately start selling.

Use the APP introduction framework whenever possible: agree with the reader's concern, introduce the solution or answer the article will explore, and preview what the reader will learn. This is a conditional framework, not a rigid sentence template or permission to promise medical outcomes.

Use descriptive, specific, reader-focused headings that answer relevant questions. Organize content into logical H2 sections. Use a clear H1/H2/H3 hierarchy as needed; H3s and a separate FAQ block are conditional on improving organization or answering reader questions. Do not require every heading level or an FAQ block in every article.

Keep paragraphs short; most should contain one or two sentences. Use bullets whenever possible to improve readability. Aim for a sixth- to ninth-grade reading level, short sentences, and easy scanning. These are readability expectations, not proof that a formula score establishes editorial quality.

Avoid generic phrases such as "In today's fast-paced world," "When it comes to," "Unlock the secrets," "Delve into," and "Game-changing." Do not copy content from competitors.

## SEO, AEO, and Geography

Use relevant topic terms naturally to explain the assigned subject and its context. Preserve the supplied title and SEO or AEO label.

Include city, state, and service area naturally when applicable. Include neighborhoods and surrounding markets when they help explain location or service coverage. Do not force geographic keywords or imply a physical location unsupported by practice evidence.

Support traditional SEO and AI/AEO discovery through direct answers and clear, supported information about relevant practices, providers, expertise, services, and geography. This does not require a separate AEO section or establish a ranking guarantee.

## Links and Closing Call to Action

Include two to four internal content links, with two for a 300-word article and four for an article of 600 or more words. The initial workload therefore requires four internal content links. The call-to-action link does not count toward that total. Prioritize the main service page, related service pages, and relevant educational articles. The first internal link should typically point to the primary service being promoted. Do not link to the same internal destination twice.

Provider, contact, and booking pages are also useful internal destinations where relevant. A contact or team link used as the call to action remains excluded from the content-link count. Choose links for reader usefulness rather than inserting unrelated destinations to meet the count.

Include one or two external links when appropriate. Prefer authoritative sources such as the American Academy of Dermatology, Mayo Clinic, NIH, CDC, FDA, and the American Society of Plastic Surgeons. These are preferred examples, not an exclusive source list. Do not link to direct competitors or directories such as Wikipedia. Competitor research does not permit competitor links in the delivered article.

End each article with a clear, customized invitation and a scheduling link and/or phone number. Direct the reader toward an appropriate consultation or booking pathway. Name relevant providers when supported and useful, under [Provider Coverage](#practice-research-and-provider-coverage); do not invent procedure associations or consultation terms to make the invitation more specific.

## Medical Claims and Optional Supporting Material

Fact check medical claims and create realistic expectations. Never guarantee results, promise outcomes, claim that treatment is risk-free, or use unsupported statistics. Use qualified language when supported, including variation between people and the role of consultation in determining candidacy. Qualifying a statement does not substitute for factual support.

Use available approved provider quotes when they add genuine expertise or perspective. Never fabricate quotations. If an approved quote is unavailable, flag a useful location through User Design's optional-material comment process.

Incorporate Facebook and Instagram into the content strategy where applicable; a social link or mention is not a mandatory feature of each article. Use Google reviews or testimonials where appropriate and useful. They must be genuine and attributable. Never manufacture review language. When relevant material is unavailable, use the optional-material comment process. Omit material that does not help this article.

A missing fact is required when the article needs it to satisfy an applicable requirement or support its central answer or a claim that must remain. When supported wording or omission satisfies the requirement without weakening the answer, use that approach. A potentially useful detail does not itself justify Needs Information status. Architecture owns the dependency assessment; User Design owns markers, comments, status presentation, and customer resolution.

## AP Style and Formatting

Follow AP Style unless the client specifically requests otherwise, with BMP's explicit conventions below.

- Spell out numbers one through nine; use numerals for 10 and above.
- Use BMP's custom state abbreviations when appropriate, such as MD, VA, and DC.
- Use the percent symbol.
- Capitalize professional titles only when they appear before the person's name.
- Use date forms such as June 15, 2026, and Monday, June 15.
- Use the Oxford comma for BMP consistency.
- Follow AP compound-modifier hyphenation.
- Format phone numbers as 301-555-1234 and verify the actual number.
- Use AP headline-style capitalization for headings.
- Spell business names correctly and preserve practice terminology.

## Editing, Verification, and Human Review

AI may assist research, brainstorming, outlining, draft generation, and phone checks. Each article must still be fact checked, edited, customized for the client, and substantially verified. Never deliver AI-generated content without substantial editing and verification.

Existing approved product exception: Alex provides human review after delivery through User Design's customer review process. Human review is not required before delivery in this product version. Customer confirmation remains open in [Roadmap](roadmap.md#open-questions-for-alex). AI review does not establish human review or customer acceptance.

Before delivery, verify the answer, accuracy, substantive length, readability, call to action, links, geography, AP formatting, Oxford commas, numbers, phone number, business name, headings, and short paragraphs. Check grammar and spelling, verify facts, complete a final proofread, and remove repetitive AI language. Engineering owns verification methods and evidence; Architecture runs the checks automatically as part of production.

Verify conditional requirements against their applicability and supporting evidence. Distinguish failed requirements, missing required inputs, unavailable optional material, and requirements that do not apply. Do not fail an article merely because an optional feature is absent. Writing Standards defines compliance; User Design defines permitted delivery with missing required facts and Alex's acceptance process.
