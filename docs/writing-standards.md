# Writing Standards

Writing Standards owns the research, content, evidence, structure, language, and formatting standards that assigned writing must satisfy. It defines the required baseline, assignment additions, applicability, and approved interpretations and exceptions.

Read the sections governing the article and its missing inputs. Product owns system purpose and assignment scope; User Design owns product-user behavior, delivery, missing-information presentation, and customer review; Data Model owns record meanings and valid associations; Architecture owns the production flow; Engineering owns verification methods and evidence; Release owns deployment and recovery; AGENTS owns agent responsibilities, authorization, and governing-document changes.

Writing Standards consolidates the company style guide and assignment requirements into the active writing specification. Architecture applies these standards during production, Engineering verifies compliance, and User Design defines how articles and unresolved information reach Alex. Archived source material preserves provenance, prompts, and examples; agents use this document without reconstructing the requirements from those sources for each article.

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Required baseline | Follow the style-guide rule with its original conditions, unless an explicit override is recorded below. |
| Required addition or override | Follow an unconditional assignment requirement or the stated replacement of a baseline rule. |
| Conditional addition | Apply when the material is relevant and supported. Assess relevance to this article rather than treating the feature as universally required. |
| Optional enhancement | Useful material that is unavailable or not needed for the article's answer. Its absence alone does not prevent Ready for Review. |

Interpret assignment additions according to their intended outcome. Preserve explicit requirements and exceptions; use the article's reader question to determine relevance. Do not turn examples or useful details into universal requirements. Conditional requirements still need an applicability decision. Record whether they apply, whether supporting material is available, and whether the article includes or omits the material.

An article-specific client instruction overrides the affected baseline rule when the instruction explicitly requests a different treatment. Keep that override distinct from the source rule. Do not silently replace the assigned topic, invent requirements, or report an unavailable input as a passed check. Resolve ambiguous instructions with the operator or Alex as appropriate under AGENTS and User Design.

## Reader Answer and Substantive Content

Baseline: write for people first, answer the reader's question clearly, accurately, and thoroughly, and demonstrate familiarity with reader concerns and expectations. Provide accurate expertise, trusted supporting information, and useful practice specifics. Avoid exaggerated claims and unsupported promises. The content must be helpful, engaging, easy to read, well structured, and encourage an appropriate next action.

Assignment override: each article in the initial workload must contain at least 1,200 words of substantive content. This replaces the guide's ranges for standard blogs, competitive topics, and local landing pages and the shorter lengths printed beside assignment prompts. The minimum is not the objective by itself. Never add filler simply to reach a word count.

Assignment addition: use the [Dontage example identified by Peter](../sources/seo-aeo/assignment.md) as a quality reference for localized, treatment-specific education, useful subtopics, natural practice integration, authoritative support, and a localized call to action. Do not copy its content or force its topic-specific structure onto another article. The product's Benchmark writer-quality goal remains owned by Product and assessed through Alex's review under User Design.

## Practice Research and Provider Coverage

Assignment addition: research the client's own website before using third-party sources. Establish the practice context relevant to this article, including providers, credentials, treatments, technology, philosophy, locations, differentiators, terminology, and client writing preferences. The article need not contain every research subject. Treat supplied reference URLs as research starting points rather than material to rewrite or the only sources to consult. Architecture defines the research stages.

Baseline reinforced by the assignment: customize each article for the actual client. Substituting practice, provider, and city names in generic copy is insufficient. Use supported details naturally to explain the topic, patient experience, or consultation choice. Preserve the correct person, credential, service, treatment association, physical location, and target geography under Data Model.

Approved interpretation of Peter's provider instruction: include providers whose supported roles or expertise help explain the topic or consultation choice. Avoid focusing arbitrarily on one person when several are relevant, unless the prompt requires that individual. This is relevant provider coverage, not a requirement for a complete practice roster or procedure-by-provider mapping. Confirm a procedure-provider association before making that claim; do not require the association when supported wording satisfies the article's purpose without it.

## Voice, Introduction, and Structure

Baseline: use conversational language, active voice, clear explanations, natural transitions, and real-world examples when appropriate. Avoid repetitive wording, keyword stuffing, generic AI phrases, unnecessary technical language, long walls of text, excessive keyword bolding, and introductions that immediately start selling.

Use the APP introduction framework whenever possible: agree with the reader's concern, introduce the solution or answer the article will explore, and preview what the reader will learn. This is a conditional framework, not a rigid sentence template or permission to promise medical outcomes.

Use descriptive, specific, reader-focused headings that answer relevant questions. Organize content into logical H2 sections. Assignment interpretation: use a clear H1/H2/H3 hierarchy as needed; H3s and a separate FAQ block are conditional on improving organization or answering reader questions. Do not require every heading level or an FAQ block in every article.

Keep paragraphs short; most should contain one or two sentences. Use bullets whenever possible to improve readability. Aim for a sixth- to ninth-grade reading level, short sentences, and easy scanning. These are readability expectations, not proof that a formula score establishes editorial quality.

Avoid generic phrases such as "In today's fast-paced world," "When it comes to," "Unlock the secrets," "Delve into," and "Game-changing." Do not copy content from competitors.

## SEO, AEO, and Geography

Baseline: place the supplied primary keyword naturally in the title, first 100 words, at least one H2, meta title, meta description, and URL when applicable. Include related supporting terms naturally to establish topic context. Do not force a keyword count or density.

Unknown keywords cannot produce a passed keyword check. The initial assignment supplies titles without explicit primary keywords. Keyword inputs and the expected metadata deliverable remain open in [Follow-Up Questions for Alex](#follow-up-questions-for-alex). Do not silently declare the title or a research query to be Alex's primary keyword.

Include city, state, and service area naturally when applicable. Assignment interpretation: include neighborhoods and surrounding markets when they help explain location or service coverage. Do not force geographic keywords or imply a physical location unsupported by the practice evidence.

Assignment addition: support traditional SEO and AI/AEO discovery through direct answers and clear, supported information about relevant practices, providers, expertise, services, and geography. This does not require a separate AEO section or establish a ranking guarantee. Preserve the assignment's SEO or AEO label; the expected difference in writing approach remains an open customer question.

## Links and Closing Call to Action

Baseline: include two to four internal content links, with two for a 300-word article and four for an article of 600 or more words. The initial workload therefore requires four internal content links. The call-to-action link does not count toward that total. Prioritize the main service page, related service pages, and relevant educational articles. The first internal link should typically point to the primary service being promoted unless it is in the keyword or keyword phrase. Do not link to the same internal destination twice.

Assignment addition: provider, contact, and booking pages are also useful internal destinations where relevant. A contact or team link used as the call to action remains excluded from the content-link count. Choose links for reader usefulness rather than inserting unrelated destinations to meet the count.

Include one or two external links when appropriate. Prefer authoritative sources such as the American Academy of Dermatology, Mayo Clinic, NIH, CDC, FDA, and the American Society of Plastic Surgeons. These are preferred examples, not an exclusive source list. Do not link to direct competitors or directories such as Wikipedia. Competitor research does not permit competitor links in the delivered article.

End each article with a clear, customized invitation and a scheduling link and/or phone number. Direct the reader toward an appropriate consultation or booking pathway. Name relevant providers when supported and useful, under [Provider Coverage](#practice-research-and-provider-coverage); do not invent procedure associations or consultation terms to make the invitation more specific.

## Medical Claims and Optional Supporting Material

Baseline: fact check medical claims and create realistic expectations. Never guarantee results, promise outcomes, claim that treatment is risk-free, or use unsupported statistics. Use qualified language when supported, including variation between people and the role of consultation in determining candidacy. Qualifying a statement does not substitute for factual support.

Assignment conditional additions: use available approved provider quotes when they add genuine expertise or perspective. Never fabricate quotations. If an approved quote is unavailable, flag a useful location through User Design's optional-material comment process.

Incorporate Facebook and Instagram into the content strategy where applicable; a social link or mention is not a mandatory feature of each article. Use Google reviews or testimonials where appropriate and useful. They must be genuine and attributable. Never manufacture review language. When relevant material is unavailable, use the optional-material comment process. Omit material that does not help this article.

A missing fact is required when the article needs it to satisfy an applicable requirement or support its central answer or a claim that must remain. When supported wording or omission satisfies the requirement without weakening the answer, use that approach. A potentially useful detail does not itself justify Needs Information status. Architecture owns the dependency assessment; User Design owns markers, comments, status presentation, and customer resolution.

## AP Style and Formatting

Baseline: follow AP Style unless the client specifically requests otherwise, with BMP's explicit conventions below.

- Spell out numbers one through nine; use numerals for 10 and above.
- Use the guide's custom state abbreviations when appropriate, such as MD, VA, and DC.
- Use the percent symbol.
- Capitalize professional titles only when they appear before the person's name.
- Use the guide's date forms, such as June 15, 2026, and Monday, June 15.
- Use the Oxford comma for BMP consistency.
- Follow AP compound-modifier hyphenation.
- Format phone numbers as 301-555-1234 and verify the actual number.
- Use AP headline-style capitalization for headings.
- Spell business names correctly and preserve practice terminology.

The [archived guide](../sources/seo-aeo/content_style_guide.md#ap-style-requirements) supplies examples and optional heading tools. Its heading and phone conventions remain part of this consolidated baseline; external tools do not establish compliance on their own.

## Editing, Verification, and Human Review

Baseline: AI may assist research, brainstorming, outlining, draft generation, and phone checks. Each article must still be fact checked, edited, customized for the client, and substantially verified. Never deliver AI-generated content without substantial editing and verification.

Existing approved product exception: Alex provides human review after delivery through User Design's customer review process. This replaces the guide's pre-submission human-review requirement for this product version. Customer confirmation remains open below. AI review does not establish human review or customer acceptance.

Before delivery, verify the answer, accuracy, substantive length, readability, call to action, applicable keywords and metadata, links, geography, AP formatting, Oxford commas, numbers, phone number, business name, headings, and short paragraphs. Check grammar and spelling, verify facts, complete a final proofread, and remove repetitive AI language. Engineering owns verification methods and evidence; Architecture runs the checks automatically as part of production.

Verify conditional requirements against their applicability and supporting evidence. Distinguish failed requirements, missing required inputs, unavailable optional material, and requirements that do not apply. Do not fail an article merely because an optional feature is absent. Writing Standards defines compliance; User Design defines permitted delivery with missing required facts and Alex's acceptance process.
