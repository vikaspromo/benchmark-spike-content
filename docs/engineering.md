# Engineering

Engineering owns implementation discipline and verification. It defines how product changes are developed, investigated, and reviewed, and which methods and evidence establish that articles and product behavior satisfy their requirements.

[Product](product.md) owns the required outcomes and scope; [Writing Standards](writing-standards.md) owns article requirements; [User Design](userdesign.md) owns the operator and customer experience. [Architecture](architecture.md#evaluate-and-revise) owns the single article evaluation and revision flow; Engineering defines how to verify its requirements without adding editorial obligations or another revision flow. [Data Model](data-model.md#assignments-and-article-status) owns assessment records. [Release](release.md) owns product deployment and recovery; [AGENTS](../AGENTS.md) owns agent responsibilities and governing-document changes.

## Article Verification

Establish whether the resulting article meets [Writing Standards](writing-standards.md), applicable assignment-specific instructions, and [User Design's presentation requirements](userdesign.md#article-content-and-writing-standards). Those documents own the requirements; verification does not add editorial obligations. [Architecture](architecture.md#evaluate-and-revise) owns when checking and revision occur.

Use a checklist covering every applicable requirement, with its conditions and exceptions intact. The full local-article checklist covers assignment and reader fit; sentence and section usefulness, voice, and coverage; factual support and associations; practice customization and clinician selection; readability; grammar, spelling, and style conventions; word count, headings, links, geography, and call to action; and required-fact markers and prepared comments. Check local presentation in a rendered preview. Use the methods below and the requirements they reference to establish each result; [Architecture](architecture.md#evaluate-and-revise) owns the initial evaluation, correction pass, and full recheck order. These checks apply to each evaluated version, including proofreading and rendered inspection. Checks that require the actual customer Doc or batch Sheet occur during delivery under Architecture.

Articles must be fact checked, edited, and substantially verified before delivery; never deliver AI-generated content without substantial editing and verification. AI may assist research, brainstorming, outlining, drafting, and phone checks. Mechanical checks may establish counts, formatting, and other directly measurable properties. Editorial assessment must establish reader usefulness, heading relevance, distinct coverage, voice, and meaningful practice customization under Writing Standards. Evidence assessment must establish factual support, correct associations, and justified medical qualifications. Proofread each evaluated version against Writing Standards, including its voice, reader fit, and writing conventions; include initial proofreading findings in the correction pass and proofread the corrected version before declaring it ready. A passed count or supported claim alone does not establish editorial quality.

Verify [Clinician Selection](writing-standards.md#clinician-selection) by counting the clinicians featured against the stated limit and checking the evidence for their experience, expertise, treatment involvement, and attributed approach. Assess whether the article gives the reader a supported reason to consider each clinician for the concern discussed.

Verify [Readability](writing-standards.md#readability) through paragraph and sentence counts and assessment of the actual language for the [Target Reader](writing-standards.md#target-reader). Perform both parts in the initial evaluation and repeat them on the corrected article under [Architecture](architecture.md#evaluate-and-revise). Count prose body paragraphs and the sentences in each, excluding headings, list items, and comments; account for abbreviations and initials when identifying sentence boundaries. Record the number and proportion of body paragraphs containing one or two sentences in the final assessment. Review whether parallel information would be clearer as bullets, and identify passages that need that change.

Assess the sixth- to ninth-grade reading-level aim, short sentences, and easy scanning while preserving useful substance. Identify passages whose sentence structure, unfamiliar terms, or unexplained concepts make them harder for the intended reader to understand, using the sentence-context checks below. A numerical reading-level estimate may inform this assessment; if used, run it during the initial evaluation and use the same method for the corrected article. Record the method and material limitations with the final result. A score alone does not establish a pass or failure; explain concrete language concerns or why the wording is appropriate for the reader. Paragraph counts alone do not establish the reading-level aim.

Verify [Reader Priorities](writing-standards.md#reader-priorities), [Article Voice](writing-standards.md#article-voice), and [Section Focus](writing-standards.md#section-focus) by reading every sentence in the completed article in its paragraph, section, and article context. Use the [Target Reader](writing-standards.md#target-reader) established for the assignment. Apply the same checks to bullets and examples, including items that are not complete sentences:

- **Purpose:** Identify what the sentence helps the reader understand or accomplish. Useful purposes include facts, explanations, comparisons, examples, necessary qualifications, and transitions that make the explanation easier to follow. Recommend removal when the content serves none of these purposes or merely announces the value of nearby content. Recommend rewriting when a useful purpose exists but the wording fails to express it.
- **Contribution:** Identify what the sentence adds in context and what useful meaning would be lost if it were removed. A sentence may apply an earlier point to a different situation or connect parts of an explanation; it need not introduce a new fact. Recommend removal when another passage already supplies its useful meaning and the repetition adds no explanation, application, or necessary emphasis. Recommend shortening or combining when only part of the passage adds value.
- **Clarity:** Establish what the intended reader can understand from the actual wording. Identify unclear terms, references, abstract phrasing, unnecessary technical language, and missing explanations. Do not treat a useful intended meaning as clearly expressed when the reader must supply that meaning. Assess whether the wording follows Article Voice, including natural, conversational language. Recommend removing an unclear clause when it adds no necessary meaning or its useful meaning is already expressed clearly nearby. Otherwise recommend rewriting or explaining the needed information; lack of clarity alone does not justify losing useful content.
- **Relevance:** Establish how the sentence develops the assigned answer and the question or subject stated by its heading. Identify advice that replaces an available answer, and check whether important facts explain a relevant result, experience, choice, or commitment when the evidence supports that connection. Recommend removal when the content does not serve the assigned answer or the reader's relevant purpose anywhere in the article. Recommend moving useful content that answers a relevant question under the wrong heading, and developing an available answer when advice has replaced it.
- **Qualification value:** Identify the specific claim, misunderstanding, or uncertainty each warning or caveat addresses. Check whether it supplies a necessary limit or useful safety information beside the affected content. Recommend removal when the qualification addresses no identifiable misunderstanding or uncertainty, repeats an established limit without adding information, or qualifies a claim the article neither makes nor implies. Preserve and clarify qualifications whose removal would make the explanation misleading or lose relevant safety information.

Then assess each section and the article as a whole. Check whether each heading receives a substantive answer, paragraphs and sections add distinct value, and the sequence develops the reader's understanding. Identify misplaced material, tangents, and overlap without useful development. For first-time treatment articles, check whether the relevant sequence is understandable and whether the evidence supports any stated practice process or visit timing. Stock-phrase searches, paragraph counts, and sentence checks alone cannot establish these results.

Support each checklist result with evidence appropriate to the requirement. For editorial results, use representative passages from the exact article to explain how the writing satisfies the requirement. Those examples illustrate the completed assessment; they do not establish a pass while concrete violations remain elsewhere. A list of present facts, links, qualifications, or clinician names is not enough to establish reader usefulness. Record concrete violations as failures with their locations and needed corrections; do not leave those violations as caveats within a passed result. Tie each recommended repair to the failed check and explain why removal, rewriting, shortening, moving, combining, or further research addresses the finding. When several checks affect the same passage, recommend a repair that preserves its useful meaning and necessary qualifications. Retain concise evidence in the final-check portion of the support-and-check record under [Data Model](data-model.md#assignments-and-article-status). A permanent justification for every sentence is not required.

Verify factual support during the initial evaluation and repeat that assessment on the corrected article. Compare the article's actual claims with the supporting source passages and context under [Data Model](data-model.md#article-research-material), including medical facts, practice policies, prices, timing, credentials, and treatment associations. Check whether wording preserves the source's conditions, exceptions, uncertainty, and scope. Do not turn ambiguous or conflicting source wording into a definite instruction or claim. Identify unsupported wording and the needed removal, qualification, clarification, or further research under [Writing Standards](writing-standards.md#medical-claims) and [Architecture](architecture.md#research-and-resolve).

For the practice-substitution test under [Customization](writing-standards.md#customization), identify actual content that needs substantive rewriting or replacement for another practice offering the same services. Distinguish that evidence from the article's general educational value; passing substitution alone does not establish useful explanations.

Use [Data Model's assessment distinctions](data-model.md#assignments-and-article-status) for passes, failures, required facts awaiting Alex, optional requests, and requirements that do not apply. An absent optional feature does not itself establish failure. Measure final body words after editorial assessment and any corrections, using [Writing Standards' Body Word Count](writing-standards.md#body-word-count) rule and counting scope. Record the count in the assessment rather than the customer article. If the final body falls below the drafting target, record the editorial reason for the reduction and evidence that the article still answers the assigned question thoroughly; do not treat the lower count alone as a failure.

Verify the exact resulting article version identified in the support-and-check record. Findings guide [Architecture's single evaluation and revision flow](architecture.md#evaluate-and-revise). Update the record with the final recheck outcome rather than retain separate correction reports. [Data Model](data-model.md#assignments-and-article-status) defines its contents and retention limits.

Before claiming delivery complete, verify that the actual customer Doc preserves the evaluated article and required comments under [Architecture](architecture.md#deliver-to-alex). Local checks establish only the local result. If delivery changes content or presentation, assess the resulting version through Architecture's evaluation flow.

Verification supports direct delivery without Vikas's article-by-article approval. It does not establish customer acceptance or assessment reliability across assignments. [User Design](userdesign.md#review-revision-and-writing-feedback) defines Alex's review and acceptance.
