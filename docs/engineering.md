# Engineering

Engineering owns implementation discipline and verification. It defines how content production product changes are implemented, investigated and reviewed, and what evidence establishes their correctness.

Read the sections governing the change and the claims being made. Product owns system purpose and scope; User Design owns product-user behavior, language and voice; Writing Standards owns article content and writing standards; Data Model owns record meanings, identity and validity; Architecture owns component responsibilities, processing and serving; Release owns deployment and recovery; AGENTS owns agent responsibilities, authorization and governing-document changes.

Engineering uses those governing requirements to establish implementation and verification expectations. It does not independently define product behavior or deployment procedures.

## Article Verification

Before delivery, fact check and substantially edit each article, and verify its quality and client customization against [Writing Standards](writing-standards.md). That document owns the active writing requirements, applicability, and approved exceptions. Verification covers the resulting text, images, and captions and must support direct delivery without requiring Vikas to review or approve individual articles.

Verify the content, SEO, formatting, and quality-control requirements in [Editing, Verification, and Human Review](writing-standards.md#editing-verification-and-human-review). Preserve each requirement's conditions and overrides. For conditional material, record relevance, availability, and disposition. Distinguish failed requirements, missing required inputs, unavailable optional material, and requirements that do not apply. Do not fail an article merely because an optional feature is absent or report an unavailable required input as a passed check.

Assess compliance, factual support, and reader usefulness separately. Counts, formatting checks, and supported claims do not establish that the article explains the topic well. Identify what the intended reader learns from each section, whether its explanations support its heading, and whether it adds information beyond earlier sections. Assess the contribution of each image and caption to the surrounding explanation. Identify tangents, duplicated answers, and material that can be removed without losing useful information. Assess whether repetition serves a clear purpose and whether consultation advice substitutes for an answer the research can support.

Compare the resulting article with the selected material and reasons for omissions in [Data Model's article research material records](data-model.md#article-research-material). Identify selected material missing from the article, material used beyond its evidence, and available relevant practice material overlooked in favor of generic explanations. Record specific findings against [Writing Standards' material priorities](writing-standards.md#practice-research-and-provider-coverage); omission alone does not establish a failure when its reason is justified.

Apply Writing Standards' practice-substitution test to the complete article. Identify the actual content or images that would need substantive rewriting or replacement for another practice offering the same services, their source evidence, and their contribution to the article. Apply the test as defined by Writing Standards rather than accepting factual rechecking or a plan to use material as proof of customization. If the resulting article does not meet the test, record failed customization and return it for research or revision under Architecture.

For each included image, compare the actual asset with its source and the material record. Verify the supported practice, person, treatment, product, or technology association and the caption's claims under Writing Standards. Check that presentation preserves relevant labels and does not misrepresent a case or imply an unsupported result. Confirm that product and technology images match the exact practice use established by the evidence. Inspect the rendered article for image clarity, readable captions and relevant labels, appropriate placement, distortion, cropping that changes meaning, and overlap under [User Design](userdesign.md#article-content-and-writing-standards).

Record defects with their locations or quoted passages, why they weaken understanding or violate a requirement, and the correction required. Correct failures and repeat affected assessments on the resulting version under [Architecture](architecture.md#evaluate-and-revise). Preserve justified medical qualifications and safety advice when cutting repetition. Do not use a passed mechanical check to dismiss an unresolved editorial or image defect. Distinguish optional improvements from failures under Writing Standards.

Record the final body-word count and whether it meets the range and counting scope in [Writing Standards](writing-standards.md#reader-answer-and-substantive-content). Record this measurement in the production assessment, not in the customer-facing article. Verify that length revisions preserve useful coverage, factual qualifications, and meaningful practice material. A count within the range does not excuse a quality failure.

Verify the actual customer Doc preserves the evaluated text, headings, links, images, captions, markers, and required comments before claiming delivery complete under [Architecture](architecture.md#deliver-to-alex). Inspect its image presentation and compare it with the evaluated version. Local document checks establish only the local result; repeat affected checks when transfer changes content or presentation.

Verification does not establish customer acceptance or the reliability of the assessment method across assignments. [User Design](userdesign.md#review-revision-and-writing-feedback) defines Alex's review and acceptance.
