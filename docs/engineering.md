# Engineering

## Article Verification

Establish whether the exact article version meets [Writing Standards](writing-standards.md), applicable assignment-specific instructions and [User Design’s presentation requirements](userdesign.md#article-content-and-writing-standards). Verification does not add editorial obligations.

Apply the local verification methods below to each evaluated version in [Architecture’s evaluation and revision flow](architecture.md#evaluate-and-revise), including proofreading and rendered inspection. Checks of the actual customer Doc and review index occur during [delivery](architecture.md#deliver-to-alex).

### Checklist and Evidence

Use a checklist covering every applicable requirement, with its conditions and exceptions intact. Articles must be fact checked, edited and substantially verified before delivery. Use mechanical checks for counts, formatting and other directly measurable properties, editorial assessment for writing quality, and source comparison for factual support. A passed count or supported claim alone does not establish editorial quality.

Support each checklist result with evidence appropriate to the requirement. For editorial results, use representative passages from the exact article to explain how the writing satisfies the requirement. These examples illustrate the completed assessment; they do not establish a pass while concrete violations remain elsewhere. A list of present facts, links, qualifications or clinician names is not enough to establish reader usefulness.

Record concrete violations as failures with their locations and needed corrections. Tie each recommended repair to the failed check and explain why removal, rewriting, shortening, moving, combining or further research addresses the finding. When several checks affect the same passage, recommend a repair that preserves its useful meaning and necessary qualifications.

Use [Data Model’s support-and-check record and assessment distinctions](data-model.md#assignments-and-article-status) to record the exact version assessed, results and supporting evidence. A permanent justification for every sentence is not required.

### Editorial Assessment and Repair

Verify [Reader Priorities](writing-standards.md#reader-priorities), [Article Voice](writing-standards.md#article-voice) and [Section Focus](writing-standards.md#section-focus) by reading every sentence in its paragraph, section and article context. Use the [Target Reader](writing-standards.md#target-reader) established for the assignment. Apply the same checks to bullets and examples, including items that are not complete sentences:

- **Purpose:** Identify what the sentence helps the reader understand or accomplish. Useful purposes include facts, explanations, comparisons, examples, necessary qualifications, and transitions that make the explanation easier to follow. Recommend removal when the content serves none of these purposes or merely announces the value of nearby content. Recommend rewriting when a useful purpose exists but the wording fails to express it.
- **Contribution:** Identify what the sentence adds in context and what useful meaning would be lost if it were removed. A sentence may apply an earlier point to a different situation or connect parts of an explanation; it need not introduce a new fact. Recommend removal when another passage already supplies its useful meaning and the repetition adds no explanation, application, or necessary emphasis. Recommend shortening or combining when only part of the passage adds value.
- **Clarity:** Establish what the intended reader can understand from the actual wording. Identify unclear terms, references, abstract phrasing, unnecessary technical language, and missing explanations. Do not treat a useful intended meaning as clearly expressed when the reader must supply that meaning. Assess whether the wording follows Article Voice, including natural, conversational language. Recommend removing an unclear clause when it adds no necessary meaning or its useful meaning is already expressed clearly nearby. Otherwise recommend rewriting or explaining the needed information; lack of clarity alone does not justify losing useful content.
- **Relevance:** Establish how the sentence develops the assigned answer and the question or subject stated by its heading. Identify advice that replaces an available answer, and check whether important facts explain a relevant result, experience, choice, or commitment when the evidence supports that connection. Recommend removal when the content does not serve the assigned answer or the reader's relevant purpose anywhere in the article. Recommend moving useful content that answers a relevant question under the wrong heading, and developing an available answer when advice has replaced it.
- **Qualification value:** Identify the specific claim, misunderstanding, or uncertainty each warning or caveat addresses. Check whether it supplies a necessary limit or useful safety information beside the affected content. Recommend removal when the qualification addresses no identifiable misunderstanding or uncertainty, repeats an established limit without adding information, or qualifies a claim the article neither makes nor implies. Preserve and clarify qualifications whose removal would make the explanation misleading or lose relevant safety information.

Assess each section and the article as a whole. Check whether each heading receives a substantive answer, paragraphs and sections add distinct value, and the sequence develops the reader’s understanding. Identify misplaced material, tangents and overlap without useful development. For first-time treatment articles, check whether the relevant sequence is understandable and whether the evidence supports any stated practice process or visit timing. Stock-phrase searches, paragraph counts and sentence checks alone cannot establish these results.

Verify [Clinician Selection](writing-standards.md#clinician-selection) by counting the clinicians featured against the stated limit and checking the evidence for their experience, expertise, treatment involvement and attributed approach. Assess whether the article gives the reader a supported reason to consider each clinician for the concern discussed.

For the practice-substitution test under [Customization](writing-standards.md#customization), identify actual content that needs substantive rewriting or replacement for another practice offering the same services. Distinguish that evidence from the article’s general educational value; passing substitution alone does not establish useful explanations.

### Factual Support

Compare the article’s actual claims with supporting source passages and context under [Data Model](data-model.md#article-research-material), including medical facts, practice policies, prices, timing, credentials and treatment associations. Check whether wording preserves the source’s conditions, exceptions, uncertainty and scope. Do not turn ambiguous or conflicting source wording into a definite instruction or claim. Identify unsupported wording and the needed removal, qualification, clarification or further research under [Writing Standards](writing-standards.md#medical-claims) and [Architecture](architecture.md#research-and-resolve).

### Readability and Proofreading

Verify [Readability](writing-standards.md#readability) through paragraph and sentence counts and assessment of the actual language for the [Target Reader](writing-standards.md#target-reader). Count prose body paragraphs and the sentences in each, excluding headings, list items and comments; account for abbreviations and initials when identifying sentence boundaries. Record the number and proportion of body paragraphs containing one or two sentences in the final assessment. Review whether parallel information would be clearer as bullets, and identify passages that need that change.

Assess the reading-level aim, short sentences and easy scanning in Writing Standards while preserving useful substance. Identify passages whose sentence structure, unfamiliar terms or unexplained concepts make them harder for the intended reader to understand, using the sentence-context checks above. A numerical reading-level estimate may inform this assessment; if used, apply the same method to each evaluated version. Record the method and material limitations with the final result. A score alone does not establish a pass or failure; explain concrete language concerns or why the wording is appropriate for the reader. Paragraph counts alone do not establish the reading-level aim.

Proofread each evaluated version against Writing Standards, including its [Article Voice](writing-standards.md#article-voice), reader fit and [Style Conventions](writing-standards.md#style-conventions).

### Word Count and Presentation

Measure body words for each evaluated version after editorial assessment and any corrections, using [Writing Standards’ Body Word Count](writing-standards.md#body-word-count) rule and counting scope. Record the count in the [assessment](data-model.md#assignments-and-article-status). If the final body falls below the drafting target, record the editorial reason for the reduction and evidence that the article still answers the assigned question thoroughly; do not treat the lower count alone as a failure.

Inspect a rendered local preview of each evaluated version against the applicable Writing Standards and [User Design presentation requirements](userdesign.md#article-content-and-writing-standards).

### Customer Delivery Verification

Verify that the actual customer Doc preserves the evaluated article’s text, headings, links, markers and required comments. Check the actual Doc and review index against [User Design’s delivery requirements](userdesign.md#assignment-delivery-and-tracking), including required presentation, access and information in the review index. Verify the recorded article status under [Data Model](data-model.md#assignments-and-article-status). A local preview, export or successful upload alone does not establish delivery.

Handle transfer or rendering failures and resulting changes to the evaluated article through [Architecture’s delivery flow](architecture.md#deliver-to-alex).

Verification does not establish customer acceptance or assessment reliability across assignments. [User Design](userdesign.md#review-revision-and-writing-feedback) defines Alex’s review and acceptance.
