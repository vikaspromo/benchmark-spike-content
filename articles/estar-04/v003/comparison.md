# Estar v002 → v003 Comparison

Revision using the same research source set and practice image, with existing sources rechecked. Governing inputs: [0607aad](https://github.com/vikaspromo/benchmark-spike-content/commit/0607aad6fd87fe32b2d765d32f4f6b7d5c9dd407). No practice blogs or new competitor search. Prior text and conversation are visible; this is not a fresh or blinded test. The held evidence-use finding was not added to guidance or the checklist. v002 remains unchanged.

Body counts exclude headings, caption, image text, comments, URLs, and Markdown syntax. Paragraph sentence counts account for abbreviations and initials. These measures confirm format changes, not editorial quality.

## What Improved

**Less advice in place of an answer.** v002 said:

> For either treatment, establish when the team expects you to assess the result and how concerns will be handled.

v003 gives supported timing:

> Estar advises allowing 10 to 14 days for the full result.

> Allowing roughly two weeks before judging the result gives recovery time to settle; individual healing may take longer.

The comparison also gives a broad 12–18-month filler duration from Cleveland Clinic, explicitly distinguished from a promised result for Estar's pictured treatment.

**More concrete practice context.** The same service source supports numbing/cold-compress options, generally up to an hour for the lip-flip visit, 15-minute observation, and lip-product/pressure aftercare. These details make the visit more tangible without presenting common care as a unique protocol.

**Easier comparison.** Event timing and treatment-specific risks have two lists. The 0.5 mL image still provides a relevant counterexample to the assumption that filler must mean a dramatic increase in size. Training is now clearly attributed to Nana Mensah instead of leaving the team sentence open to a broader reading.

## Weakest Parts

Movement versus volume is still explained several times across the opening, lip-flip, filler, and modest-result sections. Those sections have distinct purposes, but their arguments remain close enough to make the article feel more repetitive than the paragraph counts suggest.

The treatment image and published care details establish real practice material. The article still lacks an Estar clinician's explanation of why a particular person's concern led to a flip or filler. Product names and provider roles do not supply that judgment.

The original image is visually busy, with promotional hydration and booking text. Its content is preserved and legible, but it reads more like a social post than an editorial photograph; the article does not turn the hydration label into a general medical claim.

## Overall Assessment

The decision comparison is clearer and more concrete than v002, with useful practice care details and stronger scanning. Suitable to put in front of Alex under the current standards by agent assessment, but still the more repetitive of these two revisions. Its principal limit is depth and variety of explanation, not missing keywords, metadata, or a named procedure performer.
