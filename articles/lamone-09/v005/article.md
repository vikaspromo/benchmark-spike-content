## Define the Change You Want to See

[Lip filler](https://www.lamoneaesthetics.com/lip-filler-bryn-mawr-pa/) adds volume with an injectable gel, commonly made from hyaluronic acid, a substance also found naturally in the body. Placement can enhance the lip border, increase fullness, or improve differences between the upper and lower lip.

More volume and more definition are different goals. If you like your lip size but want a clearer outline, explain that distinction; a request for “fuller lips” may not express the result you have in mind.

A photograph can help communicate a preference, but your lips do not need to match someone else’s proportions. Describe which feature appeals to you, such as a defined upper-lip curve, and which parts of your own appearance you want to keep.

Filler is also different from a lip flip, which uses a muscle-relaxing injectable to reveal more upper lip without adding volume. La-Mon’e offers both, so a consultation can focus on whether your concern is fullness, shape, or the way the upper lip moves.

There is no single amount appropriate for every first-time patient. The planned result, your existing lip shape, and the chosen product determine how much filler is suitable.

## What the Consultation Needs to Establish

Before injections, a clinician reviews your goals and health and examines the area to be treated. The discussion should establish whether filler is suitable, what change is realistic, and which product and amount fit the plan.

Bring a list of medicines, supplements, allergies, and previous cosmetic treatments, including any filler you have had elsewhere. Report a history of cold sores, bleeding problems, or reactions to local anesthetics.

An active cold sore or infection around the mouth can mean postponing treatment. Pregnancy and breastfeeding also require discussion because filler safety in those situations has not been established.

Medicines and supplements can affect bleeding or bruising. Get individual preparation instructions from the clinician; do not stop a prescribed medicine simply because an online preparation list mentions blood thinners.

La-Mon’e invites patients to schedule a consultation, but its published information does not establish that consultation and injections always happen at the same visit. Confirm which appointment you are booking and whether treatment may follow the assessment that day.

Before agreeing to treatment, you should know the product, proposed cost, aftercare, and how to reach the practice with a concern. You can use a consultation to make a decision without treating it as a commitment to injections.

## What Happens During the Injection Visit

The [American Society of Plastic Surgeons explains the usual filler sequence](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/dermal-fillers/procedure): assessment and possible marking, cleansing, comfort measures, and injections. The clinician evaluates the effect as treatment proceeds rather than placing gel without checking the developing result.

Numbing cream, local anesthetic, or cooling may be used to reduce discomfort. La-Mon’e’s lip service information describes pressure or a brief pinch as possible sensations, so numbing should not be understood as a promise that you will feel nothing.

The full visit includes more than the injections. Allow time for the assessment, any numbing to take effect, and the clinician’s instructions before you leave; confirm the appointment length with La-Mon’e when booking.

Your lips can look fuller immediately, but that first appearance includes swelling. A mirror check on treatment day is useful for seeing the initial change, not for deciding that the final size is too large or too small.

## Recovery: Give the Shape Time to Settle

Swelling, tenderness, and bruising can follow lip injections. Swelling often improves within 24 to 48 hours but can last a week, and La-Mon’e advises that the final appearance develops over the following couple of weeks.

Plan your first treatment with room to recover before a wedding, photographs, or another important event. A first appointment immediately before an occasion leaves little time to see how your own lips respond.

Common aftercare includes a cold compress and a short pause from strenuous exercise. Follow the instructions you receive about lip products, touching the area, eating, and drinking, particularly while your lips are still numb.

Do not try to massage an uneven spot or reshape filler yourself unless the treating clinician directs you to do so. A concern about texture or symmetry deserves assessment, especially if it persists as swelling improves.

Filler also carries uncommon but serious risks, including blocked blood supply and tissue injury. The [FDA’s filler safety guidance](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/aesthetic-cosmetic-devices/dermal-fillers-soft-tissue-fillers) advises immediate medical attention for unusual pain, vision changes, or white, gray, or blue skin near an injection site.

Those symptoms need prompt care, not a wait for routine swelling to settle. Infection, persistent lumps, or a delayed reaction also need contact with a clinician.
