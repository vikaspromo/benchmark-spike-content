## What Lip Filler Can Change

Lip filler adds volume through injections of gel. Hyaluronic acid is a substance found naturally in the body that holds water; in filler form, it helps create fullness.

Treatment can restore lost volume, adjust an uneven shape, or define parts of the lip, including the Cupid’s bow — the curve at the center of the upper lip. You can seek more definition without aiming for much larger lips.

A reference photo can help explain your preference, but another person’s lips are not a template for your result. Your own face and starting lip shape affect what is achievable.

There is no standard amount that every first-time patient needs. Your starting lip structure, the filler type, and the amount injected affect the result.

The American Academy of Dermatology recommends a conservative approach for natural-looking results. That means avoiding a dramatic increase simply because more filler is available.

## What to Discuss Before Injections

A filler consultation generally includes a review of your goals, health history, and previous cosmetic treatments. The clinician may examine and measure your face, take photographs, and discuss the product and likely outcome.

Be specific about the change you want. For example, “I want more definition along my upper lip” gives the clinician a different starting point than “I want both lips to look fuller.”

Bring a complete list of medicines and supplements, including over-the-counter products. Mention allergies, bleeding disorders, prior injections, and cold sore outbreaks; these details can affect treatment planning.

Filler injections should wait until an active infection or skin inflammation at the treatment site is managed. The FDA also notes that filler safety is unknown during pregnancy or breastfeeding, and in patients under 22.

The specific product matters because approvals apply to particular uses. The [FDA’s dermal filler guidance](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/aesthetic-cosmetic-devices/dermal-fillers-soft-tissue-fillers) explains why patients should review the selected filler’s information rather than treat all fillers as interchangeable.

## What the Injection Visit Usually Involves

In a typical filler procedure, the clinician cleans the treatment area and may mark injection sites. Several injections can be needed to place filler in the planned areas.

Comfort measures vary. They may include a numbing cream, ice, or an injection to numb the area; some filler products also contain lidocaine, a local anesthetic.

## Swelling, Recovery, and Symptoms That Need Care

Hyaluronic acid filler can produce an immediate change, but swelling can obscure the result. Your lips may look larger or feel more tender than they will after recovery.

Redness, swelling, tenderness, and bruising are common after filler injections. The [AAD’s filler FAQs](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/wrinkles/fillers-faqs) explain that these effects often clear within seven to 14 days, sometimes sooner.

Many people resume everyday activities after treatment, but that does not mean their lips look fully settled. Leave recovery time before an important event or photographs instead of planning injections immediately beforehand.

General aftercare guidance includes:

- Wait until the next day before exercise or other strenuous activity.
- Avoid touching the treated area for three days, and massage it only if your clinician instructs you to.
- Use cooling measures as directed to help reduce swelling.

Rarely, filler can block a blood vessel and cause tissue damage, vision loss, or stroke. Unusual pain, white, gray, or blue skin near an injection site, or vision changes need immediate medical attention.

Sudden weakness or difficulty speaking also requires emergency care. Do not wait for a routine follow-up when these symptoms occur.

## How Long Results Last and How They Are Reviewed

Hyaluronic acid filler is temporary because the body gradually absorbs it. The FDA gives an approximate six- to 12-month duration for this material, but the product and treatment area affect how long the result lasts.

A follow-up gives you and the clinician a chance to assess the healed appearance and discuss whether more volume is wanted. Cleveland Clinic describes review around two weeks after lip filler; La-Mon’e’s own review schedule needs confirmation.
