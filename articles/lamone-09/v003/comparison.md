## What Improved

**More direct appointment information.** The old consultation section said:

> Do not treat the medical discussion as a formality before the cosmetic part begins.

The revision explains a concrete consequence:

> This discussion can lead to a change in timing or a decision not to proceed. An active infection or inflammation near the treatment area, for example, may need to be managed before filler is injected.

The appointment and recovery sections now separate assessment, comfort, injections, early swelling, and the settled result. General ASPS guidance supplies a 24–48-hour exercise restriction rather than leave every aftercare expectation as something to ask about.

**Clearer scanning.** Fullness, outline, and balance are grouped as distinct goals. The serious reaction signs are a separate list, while the explanation of why those signs need attention remains in prose.

**Useful practice facts remain.** The actual gallery image, treatment/dissolving prices, possible multiple dissolving sessions, deposit, and guest policy give the article material that another practice could not simply relabel.

## Evidence Corrections

v002 said La-Mon’e “uses numbing.” The source discusses what many providers do, so v003 describes anesthetic measures as a general possibility rather than a confirmed practice protocol.

v002 turned the site's ambiguous “within 48 hours” wording into a definite 48-hour cancellation-notice requirement. v003 keeps the supported deposit/guest policies and does not assert that interpretation. These are source-attribution corrections, not new writing requirements.

## Weakest Parts

The provider paragraph offers background and oversight rather than meaningful clinical judgment. It is useful context, but does little to distinguish the treatment experience. Some passages still sound like a careful patient-information guide rather than an assured editorial article; the closing consultation invitation is conventional.

The visual case supports the discussion of fullness and outline, but lacks a stated product, amount, or clinician explanation. Those limits prevent a richer case account; they do not require an invented one or prevent review.

## Overall Assessment

An improvement in clarity and direct information, with stronger formatting and better attribution. The article is suitable to put in front of Alex under the current standards by agent assessment, but its strongest customization remains the image and financial/booking details. The revision has not demonstrated deeper clinician insight or a consistently distinctive voice.
