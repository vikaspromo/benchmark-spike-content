## How Laser Skin Rejuvenation Improves Lines, Spots, and Texture

Collagen is a protein that gives skin its structure. Laser energy can stimulate the skin to make more of it, while resurfacing also helps replace damaged skin cells.

The changes you see depend on the concern being treated:

- Fine lines: Collagen renewal can make shallow wrinkles less noticeable. Improvement develops as the skin heals and rebuilds, rather than from an immediate filling effect.
- Sun spots: Treatment can reduce unwanted pigment in selected brown spots, creating a more even-looking skin tone. A spot must be correctly identified before cosmetic treatment.
- Uneven texture: Resurfacing can improve roughness and the appearance of some acne scars. It improves the skin's surface and supporting tissue; it does not promise to erase every scar.

The [American Society for Dermatologic Surgery explains](https://www.asds.net/skin-experts/skin-treatments/non-ablative-laser-resurfacing-for-wrinkles) how fractional laser treatment delivers heat in tiny columns while leaving surrounding tissue untreated. That nearby tissue helps the skin heal faster than if the entire surface were treated.

Fine lines and significant skin sagging are different concerns. Resurfacing may improve skin quality, but it cannot provide the same correction as surgery for substantial loose skin.

## What Fraxel Dual Adds to a Treatment Plan

DC Derm Docs uses the Fraxel re:store Dual system, a nonablative fractional laser. “Nonablative” means it heats tissue without removing the full outer layer of skin.

The two wavelengths allow different concerns to be addressed:

- The 1550-nanometer wavelength treats concerns including fine lines, wrinkles, and acne scars.
- The 1927-nanometer wavelength targets concerns including brown spots and freckles.

For someone with both fine cheek lines and scattered sun spots, that distinction matters: improving color and improving the skin's underlying structure are separate treatment goals. The wavelength and settings must fit the skin and the concern.

An ablative laser removes outer skin layers. Nonablative treatment generally gives subtler results with less recovery than ablative resurfacing.

## How to Protect the Improvement

Laser treatment can reduce visible signs of sun damage, but it does not stop aging or prevent future damage. The [American Academy of Dermatology recommends ongoing sun protection](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/sun-protection/sun-damage-skin/wrinkles-sun-damage-can-be-treated) because unprotected exposure can undermine treatment results and continue harming the skin.

Use broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher and a wide-brimmed hat, and limit direct sun during recovery. For a Washington, DC, commute or outdoor lunch, sun protection remains part of the plan even on cool or cloudy days.

Follow-up can assess healing and remaining concerns before you decide whether further treatment is useful.
