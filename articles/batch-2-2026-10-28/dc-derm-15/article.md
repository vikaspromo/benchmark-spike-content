## A Treatment Choice Based on the Cause of the Concern

A line, hollow, and brown spot can all make skin look older, but they do not call for the same treatment. At DC Derm Docs, Dr. Pabby observes which muscles move and identifies areas that have lost volume before choosing an injectable treatment.

That distinction helps explain the practice's different cosmetic options:

- [Botox®](https://dc-dermdocs.com/botox-dc/) temporarily relaxes targeted muscles to soften expression lines, such as frown lines between the brows. It changes the muscle activity behind those lines rather than adding facial volume.
- [Dermal fillers](https://dc-dermdocs.com/dermal-fillers-washington-dc/) add fullness and can address selected wrinkles or contours. DC Derm Docs offers several filler families, including Juvéderm and Restylane; the product and placement must suit the area being treated.
- [Fraxel Dual laser rejuvenation](https://dc-dermdocs.com/fraxel-dual/) addresses skin concerns such as uneven texture, fine lines, and selected brown spots. The practice's system uses two wavelengths, with different roles in treating texture and pigment.

Laser resurfacing can improve skin texture and color, but it cannot correct sagging skin. A plan focused on pigment or texture should be judged against those specific goals.

If frown lines and less fullness in the cheeks both concern you, Dr. Pabby may recommend starting with Botox between the brows and considering cheek filler at a later visit. The starting point depends on your features and priorities.

For expression lines, the AAD describes relaxing selected muscles while preserving movement in others. The treatment should be directed at the muscles involved in the concern rather than treating facial movement as something to eliminate.

That combination is relevant if you want help with appearance but are unsure what a mark or skin change actually is. The AAD notes that something resembling an age spot can be skin cancer; treating it as a cosmetic spot could delay the correct care.

## An Informed Plan Includes Risks, Recovery, and Upkeep

Before injections, Dr. Pabby reviews medications, allergies, and past treatments. These details help establish whether a proposed procedure is suitable and what needs attention before treatment.

The commitment varies by procedure. Botulinum toxin effects generally last about three to four months, sometimes longer, according to the AAD; fillers have different durations depending on the material and treatment area.

Filler products also have different approved uses for different injection sites. Product choice is part of the medical decision; a familiar brand name alone does not show that it is right for every area of the face.

Temporary redness, swelling, or bruising can follow injections. Fillers also carry uncommon but serious risks if injected into a blood vessel, including tissue damage, vision loss, or stroke, as explained in the [FDA's dermal filler guidance](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/aesthetic-cosmetic-devices/dermal-fillers-soft-tissue-fillers).

Unusual pain, vision changes, or skin turning white, gray, or blue near a filler injection site require immediate medical attention. These are different from routine mild swelling and should not wait for a follow-up appointment.

Laser treatment has a different recovery pattern and can cause changes in skin color. Skin tone, the device, and its settings matter, so a history of pigment changes or reactions to past procedures is relevant to planning.

Laser resurfacing may require a series of sessions over weeks or months, with improvements that develop gradually. That matters if you want a change before an event: the treatment date and the date you expect to look settled are not necessarily the same.
