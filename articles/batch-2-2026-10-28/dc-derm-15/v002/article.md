## Matching Treatment to What Is Changing in Your Face

Lines, hollows, and uneven skin texture can look like parts of the same problem in a mirror. They do not all respond to the same treatment, which makes the assessment an important part of choosing a clinician.

Before planning injections, Dr. Pabby assesses which muscles move and where volume has been lost. She and the patient then decide where to begin, rather than treating every concern as a reason for the same injection.

- **Lines linked to movement:** [Botox®](https://dc-dermdocs.com/botox-dc/) temporarily relaxes targeted muscles to soften expression lines, such as frown lines between the eyebrows. It does not replace lost facial volume.
- **Lost fullness or contour:** [Dermal fillers](https://dc-dermdocs.com/dermal-fillers-washington-dc/) can add volume in selected areas, such as the cheeks or lips. The appropriate product and placement depend on the area and the change being addressed.
- **Texture or discoloration:** A treatment directed at the skin’s surface may be more relevant than either injection.

For someone who notices both frown lines and flatter-looking cheeks, this distinction helps explain why a clinician might discuss two different tools. It also explains why having several concerns does not automatically mean treating all of them at once.

## Understanding the Commitment Before You Choose

Botox effects usually last about three to four months, sometimes longer, so maintaining the change can mean planning repeat visits. Fillers have different expected durations depending on the product; a longer-lasting product is not automatically the right match for every area.

For fillers, the [FDA identifies swelling and bruising](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/aesthetic-cosmetic-devices/dermal-fillers-soft-tissue-fillers) among common risks. Rare accidental injection into a blood vessel can cause tissue damage, blindness, or stroke, making medical judgment and familiarity with facial anatomy relevant to your choice.

Dr. Pabby reviews medications, allergies, and previous treatments before injections. Bring those details to the discussion, including the names of earlier injectable products when you know them.

Use the consultation to connect your goal with a proposed treatment, its likely benefit, and its tradeoffs. Before proceeding, establish:

- Who will perform the treatment and the total cost.
- What recovery and aftercare the proposed treatment requires.
- When follow-up is planned and how to reach the office with a concern.
