## What Long-Lasting Hair Reduction Actually Means

A laser targets melanin, the pigment in hair. Its energy becomes heat that damages the follicle, the small structure in the skin where a hair grows, and slows future growth.

That differs from shaving, which cuts hair at the skin's surface. Laser treatment aims to change how much hair grows back, rather than simply remove the hair you can see today.

Results can last months or years, but treatment does not guarantee that every hair will disappear forever. Regrowth is often finer and lighter, and occasional maintenance treatments may be needed.

The [American Academy of Dermatology's treatment overview](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/hair-removal/laser-hair-removal-faqs) explains both the lasting benefit and the possibility of touch-ups.

Hormones can also affect the result. Laser treatment reduces existing hair; it does not correct an underlying condition that causes excess growth, so new hair may continue to appear.

For women, heavy or rapidly increasing facial or body hair warrants a medical assessment because it can signal an underlying condition.

## Match the Treatment to Your Skin and Hair

Dark hair generally responds best because it contains pigment the laser can target. White, gray, blond, and red hair respond poorly, so a device's ability to treat different skin tones should not be read as a promise that it can remove every hair color equally well.

Skin contains melanin, too. For darker skin, the laser and its settings must target hair while limiting heat injury to the surrounding skin.

Experience treating your skin tone matters alongside the equipment used. Before committing to treatment, establish who will perform it, what medical supervision is provided, and their experience with patients whose skin coloring is like yours.

## Prepare Your Skin Before Each Visit

A consultation comes before treatment to assess suitability, expected benefit, and risks. Bring your medication list and mention recent tanning, a tendency to scar or form raised scars, and any history of cold sores.

Preparation generally includes:

- Avoiding sun exposure, tanning beds, and sunless tanning products. Tell the practice about a recent tan before the appointment.
- Using broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher as directed, including on exposed treatment areas during everyday outdoor time.
- Pausing waxing, plucking, and electrolysis before treatment because they disturb the hair that the laser needs to target.
- Shaving according to your treatment instructions, commonly the day before, while avoiding methods that pull hair from the root.

The pause before treatment is often four to six weeks for waxing, plucking, and electrolysis; follow the schedule provided for your plan. A beach trip or recent tan can change when treatment is appropriate, even if the appointment has been on your calendar for months.

## Know What the Visit and Recovery Involve

Everyone in the treatment room needs protective eyewear. Laser pulses may feel like brief snaps or warm pinpricks; comfort varies with the person and treatment area.

A small upper-lip area may take minutes, while a back or leg treatment can take an hour or longer.

Treated hair sheds over the following days or weeks rather than falling out all at once. Do not judge the final reduction by the appearance of your skin immediately after the first visit.

Redness, swelling, and discomfort are common short-term effects. Most people return to everyday activities, but treated skin still needs protection from direct sun and care according to the clinician's instructions.

A cool compress can ease discomfort.

Less common complications include burns, blistering, scarring, and lasting changes in skin color. Contact the treating practice for blisters, crusting, open sores, prolonged redness, or signs of infection such as pus or fever.

These reactions need assessment rather than being treated as routine shedding.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Laser Hair Removal Permanent?

It can provide long-lasting hair reduction, but it does not guarantee that all hair is gone forever. Some hair may regrow, often finer and lighter, and maintenance treatments may be needed.

### Can People With Darker Skin Have Laser Hair Removal?

Yes, with an appropriate laser, settings, and a clinician experienced in treating their skin tone. Nd:YAG is a recognized option for darker skin, but treatment still carries risks, including burns and pigment changes.

### How Many Sessions Will I Need?

Many people need four to eight treatments several weeks apart, depending on the area and response. DC Derm Docs offers six-treatment packages for specified areas, but a package does not guarantee a complete result.

### Should I Shave or Wax Before Treatment?

Shave according to your clinician's instructions, commonly the day before treatment. Avoid waxing, plucking, and electrolysis for the recommended period before treatment because they remove or disturb the hair the laser targets.
