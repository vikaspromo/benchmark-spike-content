## Semantic Differences

### Estar

Published structure separates the appointment from onset and maintenance, while our timeline section also contains numbing, observation, and aftercare. Its ordinary language makes ongoing cost easy to grasp, and its links lead to a dedicated lip-filler page and product services. Our draft gives fuller risks, qualifications, and an actual treatment image. The published article also repeats the mechanism and makes broad comfort/recovery claims; its combination advice was not independently verified in this experiment. [Published article](https://estarmedspa.com/difference-lip-flip-lip-filler-maryland/), [our article](../estar-04/v003/article.md).

### La-Mon’e

The published piece makes consultation-before-injection and appointment sequence explicit. Our article gives stronger financial, policy, image, and urgent-reaction detail. Its published opening assumes excitement and its ending uses inflated praise, which conflict with our reader/voice guidance. Outbound links lead to other treatment-selling clinics, requiring scrutiny under our source and competitor rules. Its gallery link is in the closing invitation; it does not supply four internal content destinations. [Published article](https://www.lamoneaesthetics.com/lip-filler-appointments-bryn-mawr-pa/), [our article](../lamone-09/v003/article.md).

### DC Derm Docs

The related article reaches mechanism, repeated sessions, preparation, and aftercare quickly; its closing connects treatment to relief from routine hair removal. Our article has more specific systems, package boundaries, suitability, timing, and authoritative support. Its published counterpart links to commercial clinics and has little practice-specific substance. The supplemental August posts add practical coverage and clinician attribution but also repeated reassurance and broad outcome language. ## What This Suggests for Governing Documents

### Practice Research Coverage

The clearest new process finding is that existing source selection missed Estar's [dedicated lip-filler service](https://estarmedspa.com/services/lip-filler-in-olney-maryland/). The published article's internal link exposed it. The service page gives a six- to 12-month practice estimate and distinguishes anesthetic waiting, injections, and total visit time. Our draft instead uses Cleveland Clinic's general 12–18-month duration with a disclaimer, while giving more practice detail for the flip. These ranges are not proof that one source is false; they show that our comparison did not use the available practice context for both treatments.

Recommend revising Architecture's Understand the Practice section to seek the most specific service page for each main treatment in the assignment, especially both sides of a comparison. A service overview may be a starting point rather than sufficient coverage. This does not require a website crawl, a completeness ledger, or use of the finished same-topic blog in fresh production.

### Appointment Coverage

Recommend applying Writing Standards' existing Section Focus rule more firmly. Estar's numbing/observation/aftercare belongs under appointment experience rather than the result-timeline heading. The first-time article should make the appointment sequence intelligible, while preserving uncertainty about whether consultation and treatment occur together. Do not add universal waiting times or unsupported practice protocols. Architecture already allows outline changes; no new production stage is needed.

### Voice and Useful Qualification

The useful published advantage is the ease of following the answer and connecting it to ordinary life. It is not a general advantage in factual depth, customization, or clinician selection. Our recently approved Article Voice revision already requires concrete answers, supported practical meaning, distinct development, and removal of redundant cautions. Apply that revision in the next run before adding another layer of voice instructions. Preserve relevant risks and individual limits; do not copy reassuring claims just because they appeared on the client's blog.

### Verification

The existing final checklist should verify the actual reader answer and the source support for the resulting practice claims. This experiment does not justify a required inventory of every page or discarded material. Engineering already requires representative passage evidence and treats concrete violations as failures. There is no demonstrated need for a new checklist, assessment field, independent review gate, or automated repair engine.

## Recommendation

Prioritize a small Architecture clarification about specific service-page coverage, then exercise the approved voice and clinician-selection changes in a new production run. Keep headings tied to their actual answers. No Product, Data Model, User Design, or Release change is indicated by this experiment.

Published examples can inform research and editorial judgment, but they should not become a higher authority than Writing Standards. The match limitations make cross-practice patterns provisional. No governing document or article was changed in this experiment.
