## What a Per-Unit Price Tells You

A unit measures the activity of the medicine. An injector can use several units at one injection point.

If you’re quoted a flat fee for the forehead, you’re paying for the area covered by the plan. If you’re quoted a rate per unit, the total rises with the number of units used.

For a hypothetical 25-unit quote, you’d pay $325 at $13 per unit or $375 at $15 per unit, before any minimum or separate fees. At $15 per unit, a 20-unit quote would come to $300. The lower rate in this example can still produce the higher bill if the plan calls for more units.

## Why the Number of Units Varies

Botox Cosmetic temporarily lessens activity in selected muscles. Horizontal forehead lines, lines between the brows, and crow’s feet at the corners of the eyes involve different muscles.

For example, a plan focused on frown lines and a plan that also treats crow’s feet cover different sets of muscles. The areas involved and how your muscles move help the injector decide what to include in an estimate.

Product names matter, too. Botox Cosmetic and Dysport both relax muscles, but their units measure the activity of different products.

Compare the total for the same treatment areas when the brands differ. The [Botox Cosmetic prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=485d9b71-6881-42c5-a620-a4360c7192ab) explains why the units cannot be converted.
