## What Long-Lasting Hair Reduction Really Means

Laser light targets pigment in the hair and turns into heat that damages the follicle. Unlike shaving, which cuts hair at the skin's surface, laser treatment affects the source of growth below it.

The goal is fewer hairs growing back, with regrowth often finer and less noticeable. A successful result can mean spending less time removing hair, even if occasional touch-ups remain part of your routine.

Hair color matters because the treatment needs pigment to target. Dark hair generally responds best; blonde, white, gray, and red hair respond poorly. Dark skin does not automatically rule out treatment, but it does require an experienced clinician and appropriate equipment and settings.

Hormones can also affect ongoing growth, especially on the face. Laser treatment reduces existing hair without treating an underlying cause of excess growth. A lasting result on the legs does not predict the same maintenance needs on the chin.

## Prepare Your Skin Before the First Session

Preparation begins with a consultation and a review of your skin, hair, medical history, and medicines. Mention recent tanning, a tendency to scar, cold sores, and any past reaction to a cosmetic procedure; these details can affect planning.

If you have a rash or another active skin concern in the area, raise it before treatment. DC Derm Docs also offers [general dermatology care](https://dc-dermdocs.com/general-dermatology/) for conditions such as eczema, dermatitis, and herpes, which can be relevant to that discussion.

Follow your clinician's specific instructions. Common preparation includes:

- Avoid tanning beds, sunbathing, and sunless tanning products. A tan may mean waiting for treatment.
- Use broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher when outdoors.
- Stop waxing and plucking before the treatment course as directed. These methods remove the hair the laser needs to target.
- Shave according to your appointment instructions. Shaving leaves the follicle in place while removing hair above the skin.
- Review prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines, and supplements with your clinician. Do not change prescribed treatment on your own.

For a Washington, DC, patient, planning around outdoor time is part of preparation. Tell the clinician about an upcoming beach trip or other likely sun exposure before setting dates for the treatment series.

## Plan for a Series, Not a Single Visit

Laser treatment works best on hairs in the active growth stage. Because hairs cycle through growing and resting at different times, a single visit cannot target them all.

Several sessions are usually needed, separated by weeks; the schedule depends on the area and your response.

[Mayo Clinic's overview](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/laser-hair-removal/about/pac-20394555) describes a typical course of four to eight treatments. That is general guidance, not a promise about the number you will need at DC Derm Docs.

During treatment, protective eyewear shields your eyes. You may feel brief snapping or stinging as the clinician moves across the area.

A small upper-lip treatment may take minutes, while a large area can take an hour or more.

Treated hair sheds over days to weeks rather than all falling out at once. Allow time for that change before judging the result.

## Protect Treated Skin and Recognize Problems

Redness, swelling, and discomfort are common after treatment and usually settle within a few days. A cool compress can ease discomfort, and most people can return to everyday activities without a formal recovery period.

Sun protection remains important between sessions as well as immediately afterward. Avoid direct sun on treated skin, skip tanning beds, and follow the aftercare instructions for your treatment area.

Burns, blisters, scars, infection, and lasting changes in skin color are possible. Contact your treating clinician if symptoms persist or you develop blistering or other concerning changes instead of treating them as routine irritation.
