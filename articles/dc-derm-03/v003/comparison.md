## Equipment Explanation

**Before:**

> For example, a recommendation for coarse underarm hair should address that hair and your skin, not simply repeat the benefits of the newest machine. The proposed system, settings, and expected response belong to the same clinical decision. A brand name alone is not a treatment plan.

**After:**

> Splendor X combines Alexandrite and Nd:YAG wavelengths, giving the DC Derm Docs team options for different skin and hair characteristics. That flexibility matters because the pigment in your skin affects treatment choices as well as the pigment in your hair.

The revision connects a supported feature with the reason it matters. The following list separates wavelength selection, coverage, and comfort instead of treating all features as interchangeable benefits. It does not invent a clinician’s selection protocol or explain unverified differences between the wavelengths.

## Preparation

**Before:**

> Preparation commonly includes avoiding tanning and protecting the skin from the sun. Mayo Clinic advises avoiding waxing and plucking before treatment because those methods remove the hair the laser needs to target. Shaving is generally handled differently; follow the team's instructions for when to shave the area.

**After:**

> Preparation protects the skin while preserving the hair the laser needs to target. Waxing and plucking remove that target; shaving cuts hair at the surface instead.

The following list provides the actual preparation categories, sunscreen strength, general waxing/plucking interval, and usual shaving timing. The reader receives answers, with individual appointment instructions still controlling personal preparation.

## Package Explanation

**Before:**

> Those distinctions matter more than comparing a package name or headline price alone. If your goal includes both face and neck, a full-face package does not cover the whole request. Confirm the exact area, current price, and what additional sessions would cost before committing.

**After:**

> For someone concerned about both upper-lip and chin hair, the medium option covers a different request from either small-area choice. Someone wanting face and neck treatment needs coverage beyond the full-face option.

The preceding list makes the actual boundaries easy to compare; the explanation then connects them to two concrete requests. Price confirmation is retained once rather than repeated as general advice about understanding the plan.

## Remaining Weakness

The article still lacks the practice’s own clinician reasoning, treatment case, or detailed care protocol. Its strongest practice-specific material remains the package boundaries and named systems. This revision improves the use of available evidence; it does not increase the depth of practice research or establish customer acceptance. The closing provider paragraph has supported credentials but cannot explain an unpublished clinician-to-procedure association.
